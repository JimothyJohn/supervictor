Telemetry node · dev kit → sensor hat → integrated board

One PoE cable, one clamp, nothing soldered.

An Olimex ESP32-POE-ISO powered and networked by the LS108GP switch, the DFRobot SEN0211 current clamp you already own on three jumper wires, an SHT41 on four more. The SV-HAT-A hat turns the jumpers into a plug-on board. Everything below is to scale from the Olimex drawings and the parts' datasheets; the text files next to this page are what a vendor builds from.

Needed from you

Everything a session cannot do: decisions, purchases, hands on the bench, and accounts. In the order they block.

Decide blocks firmware

  • Current register full scale: keep 6 A or go to 20 A?The 20 A probe is linear to ~17 A on the ESP32 ADC; 6 A pins at 255 above 6 A. Decide before real bursts are stored. HARDWARE.md §11
  • Power threshold for kit devices: 1000 mA?The endpoint default of 100 mA is below the clamp's ~1 A floor. Per-device field already exists.
  • Calibration gain: compile-time env or NVS?Compile-time matches how everything else is baked; NVS survives OTA. Pick one.
  • Which scope to RFQ: hat (B) only, or hat + integrated node (C)?Recommendation is B now, C at 25+ units. SOURCING.md §1

Buy ~$30 + optional meter

  • Adafruit 5776 SHT41 STEMMA QT, $5.95SHT40 (4885) is out of stock; same address 0x44, same driver.
  • Adafruit 4397 JST SH → female sockets, $0.95Plugs the SHT41 straight onto the UEXT pins.
  • Klein 69409 line splitter, $19.99 at Lowe'sOnly if the test machine is on a cord. Extech 480172 is the alternate.
  • True-RMS clamp meter if none on the benchFluke 323 $207.99 or Klein CL390 ~$100. The calibration reference.
  • For the hat build: nothing yetTurnkey assembly sources the 22 parts; the vendor quote comes first.

Bench hands required

  • Wire the SEN0211 to the POE-ISOGND → EXT1-3, VCC → EXT1-2 (+3.3 V), signal → EXT2-6 (GPIO32). 2D view below shows the pins.
  • Confirm the module reaches 2.45 V on a 3.3 V supplyIts LDO is 3.0 V with 0.3 V headroom. 1 V RMS into the jack, read TP/ADC. Decides whether the hat keeps a 3.0 V rail.
  • Clamp a known load and calibrate onceResistive heater via the splitter; meter on the same conductor. Sets the gain constant.
  • Measure the SEN0211 module's own drawNot on DFRobot's wiki. Needed for the hat power note.
  • Cable-pull fault test and a 24 h soakTODO.md P2; still outstanding since July.
  • Record which board is on which switch portThe inventory's switch_port column is empty.
  • Check the SJ1-3523N jack pin numbering against your SEN0211 plugContinuity: tip = signal, sleeve = GND. The hat netlist assumes it.

Admin vendor-facing

  • Fill the bracketed parts of the RFQ cover emailName, company, phone, shipping address, call availability. RFQ-prototype.md §12
  • Send to three shops and take two callsSierra Circuits, CircuitHub (hat); Cardinal Peak or DMC (node). 3–5 h over two weeks.
  • Open DigiKey/Mouser in a browser and confirm eight part numbersThey block automated readers; the numbers in bom-devkit.csv are exact search strings.
  • Optionally install KiCadbrew install --cask kicad lets a session turn hat/ into Gerbers here instead of paying a shop for layout.
  • Sign the PO and the 50/50 NRE termsOnly after quotes. Nothing is committed today.

Why these

BoardPOE-ISO: 3000 V isolation, no PSRAM pin remap, 802.3af, four on the rack
ClampSEN0211: burden built in, DC out, zero external parts, already owned
Temp/RHSHT41: one I²C transaction, CRC, board pull-ups already there
LinkWired only: one cable, no credentials, no WiFi wedge
Kit cost from scratch≈ $60 per node + switch port
Hat (SV-HAT-A)22 parts, 2 layers, 28 × 40 mm, ≈ $40–60 each at 25
